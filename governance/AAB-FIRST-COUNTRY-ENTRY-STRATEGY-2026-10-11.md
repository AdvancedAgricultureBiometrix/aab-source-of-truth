# AAB First-Country Entry Strategy — 2026-10-11

**Status:** PROPOSED STRATEGY RECORD — NOT AUTHORISATION  
**Origin:** Platform Owner / ChatGPT discussion, followed by Claude Code read-only repository review  
**Scope:** Direction for future first-country market entry; subject to Platform Owner approval and existing canonical controls.

This is a proposal for preservation and review. It grants no commercial, country, data-processing, invitation, admission, commissioning, procurement or deployment authority. It does not amend any existing contract or gate.

## Governing principle

AAB should enter its first country through a genuine, identified scientific research need, an appropriate institution and a bounded, independently verifiable demonstration—not through broad sales or premature national deployment.

The first country is **not selected**. No institution, funder, commercial partner or research engagement is committed.

## Staged approach

1. **Verify the technical and governance foundations.** Complete the required independent dependency-audit and RD-01 amendment reviews, resolve RD-03/RD-04 and other applicable gates. Do not represent designed or simulated capabilities as implemented or commissioned.
2. **Listen without data (may run in parallel with verification).** Ask researchers and research programme leaders about unresolved questions, evidence fragmentation, contradictory findings, cross-disciplinary barriers and the conditions required for trust. Identify the actual institutional and country decision-makers. Do not request or receive confidential research data, give platform access, promise delivery or make commitments.
3. **Demonstrate honestly.** After the required verification and implementation work, produce a governed AGR demonstration using synthetic data only: evidence provenance, scientific memory, a structured contradiction, an evidence gap, explained reasoning and an output for qualified human assessment. Existing SCS/CAP-34 demonstrations, if shown, must remain explicitly labelled as simulations with no admission or commissioning credit.
4. **Explore bounded institutional discovery.** Once an appropriate capability and authority basis exists, consider a paid or funded discovery engagement under the existing commercial pricing principles (Offer 1). Discovery alone confers no scientific, admission or compliance authority. Any formal engagement needs its own approval.
5. **Consider a real-data pilot only after all gates.** Require the first-country joint admission authority, independent and scientific reviewers, Decision 72 confidentiality/isolation/authority/recoverability safeguards, Gate D, and a country deployment agreement identifying countryAuthority and tenantOwner. External invitations remain locked until separately authorised.
6. **Expand only on demonstrated value.** Evaluate scientific utility, reproducibility, human review, institutional benefit and economic/environmental outcomes before broader country participation.

## Choosing the first opportunity

- Select the **scientific problem and institution before the country** where possible, but preserve the canonical country-request, Platform Owner decision, country Head Admin and country-tenancy journey.
- Assess research need, availability of authorised sponsors, institutional capacity, national law, practical cost, sovereignty compatibility, and feasibility of an independently credible first demonstration.
- Consider an Australian institutional or Western Australian first step alongside international options, without assuming it is automatically preferable.
- Reconcile any proposal for an AGR-led Philippine or other new entry market with the existing SCS Thailand/Vietnam entry-market record; do not silently change domain or market priority.
- Do not infer institutional interest from public programmes, website traffic or social-media interactions.

## Standing boundaries

- **Sovereignty:** One isolated country tenancy under the approved control-plane architecture. Respect the non-return boundary, egress controls, country ownership, prohibition on unauthorised cross-country movement and external AI training.
- **Honest capability status:** Explicitly distinguish design, synthetic simulation, implementation, admission and commissioning. At the time of this record, nothing is admitted or commissioned.
- **Separated roles:** Record requesting, contracting, paying, beneficiary, country-authority and tenant-owner roles independently. A funder or commercial intermediary is not automatically a country authority.
- **Institutions:** International research organisations are not automatically founding national institutions. Review data-sharing/publication requirements, legal status and institutional authority.
- **Partners:** A local development/distribution partner may be useful once a real opportunity is defined. Any later agreement must disclose conflicts, prohibit inducements, record fees/subsidies, avoid automatic exclusivity or IP transfer, and grant no data access, scientific authority or sovereign decision-making power.
- **External outreach:** Initial listening is non-confidential, non-committal and does not offer access, work, money or formal roles.

## Open decisions and exit criteria

1. Which scientific domain, research question and country should be first, and how does this align with existing SCS market records?
2. Should the first anchor be a national institution, an international research organisation, or an Australian research partner?
3. Which authorised representatives must participate, and who can approve institutional and country commitments?
4. What national data-protection, public procurement, research ethics, IP, publication and language requirements apply?
5. What objective criteria establish success or require ending a country pursuit (e.g., no authorised sponsor, no permitted data pathway, unresolvable sovereignty conflict, inadequate technical demonstration, or no credible institutional benefit)?
6. When, if ever, should a commercial country-development partner be appointed?
