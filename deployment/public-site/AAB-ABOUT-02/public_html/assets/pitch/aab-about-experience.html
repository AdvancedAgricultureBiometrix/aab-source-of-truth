Before the country scan · What is AAB?

AAB connects what a country knows, what it has, what it does not yet know — and what may be worth investigating next.

Research, field evidence, waste, resources, compliance, regulation, failed trials and scientific discoveries are often separated. AAB is governed scientific intelligence infrastructure designed to connect that learning without taking control away from people.

Why I created AAB

AAB did not begin in a boardroom. It began with what I had seen — and with questions I could not ignore.

I have travelled throughout Australia, Thailand, Vietnam and Indonesia, and worked in Africa and Papua New Guinea. Those experiences showed me how closely agriculture, water, the environment, knowledge and human opportunity are connected.

David GoreyFounder · AAB

I am not a corporate person or a scientist. I am a practical person who enjoys difficult problems. AAB began in agriculture because agriculture touches almost everything — food, livelihoods, soil, water, climate, science and national resilience. I began asking whether technology could preserve hard-earned learning, connect evidence and reveal opportunities people might otherwise miss, while scientists, institutions and countries always remain in control.

Why AAB is needed now · A national scientific opportunity

Countries cannot control every global disruption — but they can become better prepared, more scientifically connected and less dependent.

Fertiliser insecurity. Fuel and freight pressure. Fragile supply chains. Different national rules, repeated evidence requirements and limited cross-border recognition. Underused local resources and hard-earned scientific learning that can be difficult to reuse. AAB helps authorised people connect what normally sits apart.

5 · Waste can become a scientific question

Discarded glass is not automatically an ingredient. It is a material worth asking better questions about.

AAB separates what is known from what must still be proven: composition, contaminants, particle safety, plant-available silicon, soil response, processing cost and regulatory suitability.

The first discovery is not the product. The first discovery is the possibility.
